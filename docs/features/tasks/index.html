Tasks

View as markdownOpen the .md copy of this page

Tasks lists what you owe: the issues assigned to you, the pull requests to review, the messages to answer, and the lines you write yourself. Open any of them and run one of your automations on it. Tasks belongs to the workspace picked on the rail.

The four sections#

SectionHoldsFrom
To-dosLines you wroteSlate only (To-dos, reminders and meetings)
IssuesIssues and tickets assigned to you, and unassigned issues on repositories you ownJira, Linear, GitHub, GitLab, Azure DevOps, Asana, ClickUp, monday.com, Notion, Trello, Shortcut, YouTrack, Basecamp, Todoist, Plane
ReviewsReviews asked of you, pull requests assigned to you, your open ones, and unclaimed ones on repositories you ownGitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Azure DevOps, Gitea, Gerrit
MessagesChat and email waiting for your answerSlack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Google Chat, Zoom, Mattermost, Zulip, Rocket.Chat, Gmail, Outlook

How each tool is read, and how to change what it sends, is in Where tasks come from.

Reviews is grouped by the pull request's state: To review (asked of you or assigned to you), then your own: Changes asked, Checks failing, Ready to merge, Checks running, Waiting on review; and last On repositories you own, for pull requests nobody took. Issues is grouped by each tool's own status names.

The side panel#

  • Overview: its number is what needs you now: a review asked of you, a direct message, a reminder that rang.
  • To-dos, Issues, Reviews, Messages, each with a count.
  • The + in the title row adds a to-do.
  • The sync line says when your tools were last read (Synced 2 min ago), or which tool needs sign-in (Jira needs sign-in). Click it to read your tools now.

A task with an agent run working on it shows a live dot and its step on its row, such as Reviewing or Building. Needs you (amber) means it waits on you, Stopped that it stopped.

Above a section's list, one chip per tool filters to that tool. The ⋯ beside the chips offers Manage in Connect, and Disconnect <app> for an app connected from Slate or Hide from Tasks for any other. A hidden app is listed in Connect → Integrations, with Show. A tool Slate cannot reach keeps its last tasks, under Cannot reach <tool>.

Drag the line between the list and the open task to widen the list, from 320 to 640 px. Double-click the line to reset it.

The Overview#

The Overview draws your work as one picture: your tools on the left, each with what waits there and Sign in when it needs it, You wrote for your to-dos, and Connect an integration last; then the four sections; then Slate, with how much needs you. Click any of them to open its list. When your calendar is connected, today's meetings sit above it (Today's meetings). Click a tool to choose what it sends (Choose what Tasks shows).

On a first day, with nothing connected, the Overview shows the most used integrations and See all N integrations in Connect →. A tool your agents already reach is offered as Use from your agents.

A task#

Click a row to open it beside the list. It shows where it came from ("GitHub assigned this to you"), its comments and project, and:

  • Slate remembers: up to three memories about the task, first those that name its key (PROJ-123) or pull request number, then those about the same topic in its project.
  • Run <automation>: runs one of your automations on it (Run an automation).
  • ⋯: Open in <tool>, Remind me…, Change what <tool> sends, Stop showing these, and Hide from Tasks.

Hide from Tasks takes the task off this list only; nothing changes in its tool. It comes back if the tool reports a newer change. Each section has a Hidden (N) line with Unhide.

The description and comments stay in the tool: open the task there to read them.

Run an automation on a task#

Tasks runs your automations; the automation holds the process. The Run <automation> button names the automation, such as Run Fix a bug. Its chevron lists the automations that take this kind of item, yours first, then Slate's starters, and the agent to use for this run. The first run of a starter adds it to Automations.

The run happens in a session you can see, and stops for your approval before it plans and before it ships. See Agent runs and Which automation runs.

When your pull request still waits for a review or for checks, one line says there is nothing to run yet.
