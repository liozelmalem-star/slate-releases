Workspaces & Invites

Signing in

Settings → Account. Sign in with GitHub, the only provider offered.

Slate opens your system browser. After you approve, the provider returns to a loopback address that Slate's local server listens on. The server does the code exchange, so no code, token or password passes through the UI. The session is written to cloud-session.json, a 0600 file in the app-data folder, never inside a database, so exporting a workspace cannot hand over a refresh token. The local server owns it because the MCP sidecar needs the same session.

Your first sign-in creates a personal account. Everyone signs in on first launch (ADR 0103). After that, Personal works offline and never syncs until you share it.

Settings → Account shows who you are and holds Sign out. Below that, Plan shows two cards.

  • Free is everything Slate does on this Mac. It is your plan unless you have early access.
  • Team is sharing a workspace with your team. It is coming soon and has no price. Tell me when it's ready records your interest, and the button then reads We'll email you. With Team early access, the card says Early access.

Your work stays on this Mac on every plan. The account holds only who you are and your plan.

Sharing is Team. On Free, a new workspace is local only. The sharing switch is off and disabled, with a Team · coming soon note and the same button. A workspace already shared can still be turned off. With Team early access, sharing works.

Two tiers

TierMeaning
AccountThe billing and organisation tier: a company, or one person. Roles: owner, admin, member.
WorkspaceOne store with one audience, inside an account. Roles: owner, editor, viewer.

Workspace membership is independent of account membership. A contractor gets one workspace and not the company. An account owner can reach a workspace inside the account without being added, so nobody is locked out of their own company.

Settings → Workspaces lists every workspace you belong to, across all accounts.

What a role controls

A role gates administration and whether you can write at all.

RoleReadWrite docsInvite, remove, renameGrant owner, or delete the workspace forever
owneryesyesyesyes
editoryesyesnono
vieweryesnonono

The account's owner can do everything a workspace owner can. The account's admins can invite, remove and rename, and no more. Only a workspace owner or the account's owner can make, change or remove an owner, or delete the workspace forever. An account admin cannot make themselves an owner. The database enforces this.

A role does not decide whose docs you may edit. That is author-only for everyone.

A viewer's replica is read-only on your machine too. Postgres would refuse a viewer's rows on the way up, and you would believe the workspace had your docs while nobody saw them. So the role is stored locally and the write is refused where it is made, with a line saying an editor or the owner can change it. A viewer's machine does not push.

Accepting an invitation fetches your role at once. The same refusal applies to a memory you or an agent tries to save. Until the role has been fetched (offline, signed out, or a machine that joined earlier) the workspace counts as writable, because refusing on something never fetched would break offline use. The cloud still has the last word.

Inviting someone

Settings → Workspaces, click the workspace's row, open Members, type an email address and press Create invitation. The invitation is for the editor role. Slate sends no email. You send it yourself from the pending row (see Telling them).

An invitation is a membership addressed by email, because a user id exists only after that person has signed in once. The address is stored lower-cased and trimmed, enforced by the database, so it always matches a login.

Accepting is explicit. A pending invitation shows as a banner at the top of the app, whichever screen you are on. It names the workspace, who invited you and the role, with Accept and Decline. Accepting sets up the replica and the workspace appears in the switcher. A declined invitation stays declined until you are invited again.

The banner is in the shell because Settings is where nobody looks unprompted.

Pending invitations are listed with members and can be revoked before they are claimed.

Telling them

Slate has no mail server. Each pending row has two actions:

ActionWhat happens
EmailYour mail app opens with the invitation addressed and written. You send it.
CopyThe same message goes to your clipboard, for Slack, Teams or anywhere else.

The message names the workspace, says who invited them, links to where Slate is installed from, and states the exact address the invitation is tied to. Membership is claimed by email, so someone signing in with another account would find nothing and not know why.

The mail arrives from you, so the recipient recognises the sender.

Creating and deleting a workspace forever forever

New workspace in Settings → Workspaces asks for a name, a colour and whether to share it. Create workspace makes it. Sharing starts on and needs sign-in. Off, the workspace is a file on this machine and can be shared later. A shared one belongs to your account, you are its first member, and its replica is set up as part of creating it.

Sync now sits beside it and reports what moved. An action's result shows as a status note under the buttons, in its own colour.

Each workspace has a colour, used by the switcher and by the Sessions screen, which paints every place, limb and node in the colour of the workspace it runs in. Nine are offered: the mark's five (blue, teal, green, amber, red), which new workspaces get in that order, and cyan, purple, pink and lime. Under Onyx and Graphite, the marks turn grey and each workspace mark carries a small dot in its own colour instead. The colour is stored on your machine, not on the shared row. Everyone keeps their own, and anyone can recolour whatever their role.

Delete workspace forever is under General in the workspace dialog. It removes every doc everyone put there, for every member, with no notice. Authors keep what they moved back out. Only a workspace owner or the account's owner can do it, and Slate asks twice. For anyone else it does nothing.

Your own replica is marked read-only, not removed. You can still copy content out, and removing it is your decision.

Removing someone

Removing a member revokes their read access on their next request. Row Level Security decides on every query, so no cached grant remains.

On their machine, the workspace leaves the cloud's list and the next launch marks the replica read-only. They can still read and export what is on their disk. Nothing more arrives or goes up.

Docs they authored stay in the workspace. Erase my work tombstones every row they authored, and every member's replica drops its copy on the next pull.
