Features

Workspaces

A workspace is a whole store: one SQLite file on your machine holding its own pages, memory, activity and search. Switch into one and the entire app is in it.

Every workspace starts private. Share one and everything inside it belongs to its members — nothing is marked, and there is no page-level rule to get wrong.

Personal, and the ones you share

Personal is the workspace you start with, and the only one that fills without being asked: sessions captured from disk, repos with no project page, cross-project notes. It is also the store that always works — it cannot be left or deleted.

Personal can be shared like any other workspace; every row carries the same Share switch. Because sharing publishes what the store already holds, Share first counts the pages, memory atoms and activity rows it would publish and asks you to agree to that number. Switching sharing off erases them again, but they were readable in between.

Every workspace is on the same schema. That sameness is the point: the real page tree, the real editor, the real Memory graph, the real Activity dashboard and the real search all work in a shared workspace, because it is the same kind of thing they already worked in.

The switcher sits at the top of the rail and is always visible. Work landing in the wrong workspace is a privacy incident rather than a bug, so the question am I in the right workspace? has to answer itself at a glance. The switcher is the workspace's badge: Slate's mark in the workspace's own colour, a shade per leaf, above Sessions. Hover it for the name. A shared workspace's badge carries a small people mark in its corner, a workspace that is failing to sync carries a warning, and a read-only one a padlock. In the open switcher every row also says Shared or Private — that is whether the workspace syncs, not its name, so your Personal workspace reads "Personal · Private".

Switching resets the route to the Console, and the tab strip comes back as that workspace's own. A page id only means something inside one store, so carrying a tab strip across a switch would open pages that do not exist there.

What follows a switch, and what does not

Everything about the work is per workspace, the screen state included: the open tabs, your favourites and recents, which sections are expanded, what the Console is scoped to.

Some things describe this computer, not an audience, and are identical in every workspace: your theme, the sidebar and rail widths, which AI sessions are running, and the CLI accounts your tools run under. They live in a separate machine-level store, so they cannot be duplicated per workspace and disagree.

Routines are not in that list: a routine is scoped to a project page, so it lives in the same store as the project it reads.

The moving parts

PieceWhat it is
AccountThe billing tier. A company, or one person — your first sign-in gets you a personal account with one member.
WorkspaceOne store with one audience, inside an account. You can belong to many, across accounts.
ReplicaThe local SQLite file for a shared workspace. Where you read and write; the cloud rows follow.
AuthorWho wrote a row. An attribute, shown as an avatar.

A project you created can hold a repo page a teammate created. parent_id lives on the child, so creating a child is not writing the parent — which is how one shared tree composes with one writer per page.

Using it

Sign in. Settings → Account, with GitHub or Google. Your first sign-in provisions an account.

Make or join a workspace. Settings → Workspaces is where workspaces are run: create one, share one, leave one, manage its people, rename it, recolour it, delete it. A workspace you belong to but have not materialised on this machine is offered with Add here rather than provisioned silently.

Switch into it. The switcher at the top of the rail. Everything below it re-roots.

Work. Normally. Pages, the block editor, projects, repos, Memory, Activity, search and the AI in the Console all read and write the workspace you are standing in.

Move something in. Right-click a page in the sidebar — or use the ⋯ in its header — and pick Move to <workspace>. See Working in a Shared Workspace.

Read what others wrote. Their pages sit in the same tree as yours, with their avatar on the row and the editor read-only.

Ask your AI. Slate's MCP server offers recall_workspaces and workspace_page, so Claude Code, Cursor, Codex or Gemini can answer "has anyone already solved this?". recall stays inside the workspace the working directory resolves to; recall_workspaces is the separate verb for reaching across every workspace you belong to, including ones you have not provisioned here.

How a shared workspace stays in step

The background loop syncs every 2 minutes, plus once at startup. Sync now in Settings → Workspaces does it immediately and reports what moved.

A timer rather than a hook on the write path, on purpose. Pages are written from several places — the UI over HTTP, an AI tool calling MCP, the session watcher — and instrumenting each one would be three chances to miss a case. A poll cannot miss a writer it has never heard of.

It is cheap because the diff is cheap: the pass asks the cloud for two columns per row, compares a hash computed over exactly the fields that get published, and sends only what changed. A workspace with nothing new costs one query and no upload.

The same comparison is what withdraws. A row the cloud holds that your replica no longer does — deleted, archived, or moved to another workspace — is tombstoned upstream, so every member's copy goes on their next pull.

Offline works and needs no sequencing. You are the sole writer of your rows, so a queue that drains in any order lands the same state.

What actually leaves your machine

Everything in a shared workspace is meant for its members — that is what putting it there means. What is withheld is the machine-specific and the ambient:

SentNot sent
Page title, block content, and its plain-text rendering so the cloud can index it — as written, see the note belowproperties — the JSON bag of typed and captured metadata, including a project's folder path
doc_type, the parent link, and your timestampsposition — the fractional sibling index, which only means something beside the siblings you own

Git activity: the kind, the branch, a commit's subject line and message body, and the repository's normalised git remote, so a teammate's checkout at a different path matchesThe activity payload — per-file paths and diff stats — and activity.repo, the absolute path on your disk
That a session happened, with its branch and timeEverything else about it. The payload column is not published at all, so no transcript, tool or summary leaves
Your display name, email and avatar from the sign-in providerAnything in a workspace you have not shared. Anything in another workspace.

The kind list is enforced by a CHECK constraint in Postgres, not by the client. The boundary is in the database, which is the only place it cannot be forgotten.

The right-hand column withholds fields, not words. A page body and an atom's sentence are sent as written, so a filesystem path you typed into a page — or one an AI tool recorded inside a decision — goes up with the row. What is withheld is the structured place a path is held: properties, and the absolute repo path on a commit.

Every workspace, Personal included, keeps an append-only log in its own store: pages created, renamed, moved, archived and deleted, joins, syncs that moved something, and withdrawals. Two SQLite triggers enforce it — an entry cannot be changed, and cannot be deleted for 400 days.

Leaving, erasing, and being removed

Four different things, deliberately named apart — confusing them costs somebody either their access or their content.

ActionWhat happens
Stop sharingThe workspace stops publishing entirely and everything you authored is erased from the cloud. The store and all its contents stay on this machine and keep working.
LeaveThis machine's replica is deleted. Your rows upstream are untouched, so the workspace still shows them. If an AI tool connected to Slate still has the workspace open, leaving is refused and nothing changes — quit those tools and leave again. Personal cannot be left.
Erase my workEvery row you authored in that workspace is tombstoned upstream, so every member's replica drops its copy on their next pull. This is the Article 17 request.
Someone removes you, or the owner deletes the workspaceRow Level Security stops answering on your next request. Your replica is marked read-only rather than vanishing: what you could read yesterday stays readable and exportable, and removing it is your decision.

Personal is the one exception: it is never marked read-only, even if it is shared and its cloud side goes away, because every window falls back to it and nothing in the product clears the mark. Losing the cloud side there shows up as sync failing, and Stop sharing is the way out.
