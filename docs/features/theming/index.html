Features

Themes

Slate has four themes, two dark and two light. Choose one in Settings → Appearance. It sets the light/dark mode and the whole surface palette at once.

ThemeModeLook
StrataDarkThe default. Near-black ground with the wordmark's hue at low chroma
ChalkLightStrata's counterpart: the same hue, on the light curve
OnyxDarkMonochrome. True black, everything above it in grey
GraphiteLightMonochrome. Off-white paper, near-black ink

Strata and Chalk carry the five colours of Slate's mark. Those colours tell doc types, memory kinds, memory-map dots and doc covers apart.

Onyx and Graphite remove every hue, the mark's five included. The five become five steps of grey. Slate's logo follows the theme, and so does the rail's icon for where you are. Five things to know:

  • Five greys are harder to tell apart than five colours. Each mark sits beside its label, so the grey is a second cue. A monochrome memory graph is flatter.
  • State keeps its colour. Amber means something needs you, red means it failed or the next step cannot be undone, green means done or connected. These are the only colours left on screen.
  • Each workspace mark gets a small dot in the workspace's colour, since the mark itself is grey. It hides on Sessions and Settings, where the rail's mark is grey in every theme.
  • The green running light stays green. The dot for a session or automation working now has no label beside it, so in grey it could not be seen.
  • Terminal output stays in colour. A CLI's red means a failure. The terminal takes its surface from the theme and keeps its sixteen ANSI colours under all four.

Light and dark with ⌘⇧L

⌘⇧L, or Toggle light / dark theme in the palette, flips light and dark. On Onyx it goes to Graphite, and back, so the monochrome look stays. On Strata and Chalk it flips the mode only, so the Active card in Settings → Appearance does not change. To change the pair, pick the other theme there.

Accent

Buttons, links and focus rings use the mark's blue on Strata and Chalk, and grey on Onyx and Graphite. It is not a setting. Blue is the only one of the five that reads as a button on both light and dark. On Onyx the main button is a lifted dark grey with white words, and on Graphite it is black. A switch, checkbox or radio button that is on is drawn white on Onyx and black on Graphite, so on never looks like off.

There is no per-element colour picker and no custom theme file.

Size

⌘+ and ⌘− zoom the whole app in ten-percent steps between 80% and 140%, starting at 110%. Text, icons, spacing and the memory graph move together. The zoom is saved on this computer, not in your workspace.

Inside a terminal the same keys resize that terminal's font only. Click outside it and they zoom the app again.
