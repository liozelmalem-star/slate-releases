Themes

Slate ships four themes, two dark and two light, chosen in Settings → Appearance. Picking one sets both the light/dark mode and the whole surface palette, and the change applies across the app immediately.

ThemeModeLook
StrataDarkThe default. A near-black ground carrying the wordmark's hue at low chroma
ChalkLightStrata's counterpart — the same hue, on the light curve
OnyxDarkMonochrome. A true black background, everything above it in grey
GraphiteLightMonochrome. Off-white paper, near-black ink

Strata and Chalk are the coloured pair: alongside the surface they carry the five colours of Slate's mark, which are what tell doc types, fact kinds, memory-map atoms and page covers apart.

Onyx and Graphite take the colour out. They are the same two themes with every hue removed, the five of the mark included. The categorical distinction survives as five steps of grey, and Slate's own logo follows the theme rather than staying the one coloured object on the screen.

Two things are worth knowing before you pick one:

  • Five greys are harder to tell apart at a glance than five colours. Every one of these marks sits beside its own label, so the label identifies it and the grey is a second cue — but a monochrome memory map is a flatter picture than a coloured one.
  • The green running light stays green. The small dot that says a session or a routine is working right now keeps its colour under Onyx and Graphite. That dot has nothing written beside it, so in grey it was still drawn and could no longer be seen.
  • Terminal output stays in colour. A CLI's red means the build failed, and no shade of grey means that. The terminal takes its surface from the theme and keeps its sixteen ANSI colours under all four.

Accent

The accent is which of the mark's five colours leads buttons, links and focus rings. It is a separate choice from the theme, so switching between light and dark keeps it.

A monochrome theme has no hue to lead with, so the picker is replaced by a note while one is selected. Your choice is kept, not discarded — pick Strata or Chalk again and it comes back.

Appearance is a choice among designed themes. There is no per-element colour picker and no custom theme file.

Size

⌘+ and ⌘− make the whole app bigger or smaller, in ten-percent steps between 80% and 140%. It is a real zoom, so everything moves together — text, icons, spacing, the memory map — and it is remembered on this computer, not in your workspace.

Inside a terminal the same two keys resize that terminal's font first, and leave the rest of the app alone. Click outside it and they go back to the whole app.
