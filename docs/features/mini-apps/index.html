Mini apps

A mini app is a small app that runs inside Slate: a review queue, a weekly chart of your sessions, a form that files tasks. You describe it, your AI tool writes it, and it appears beside the chat that made it. You can change it by asking again, or by editing its files.

A mini app is an artifact. It lives in the Artifacts tab, under Mini apps, with every version kept. The decision behind it is ADR 0077.

Making one

  1. In Artifacts, choose New mini app.
  2. Say what it should do, in your own words.
  3. Your project's AI tool builds it. The chat on the left tells you what it is doing, and the app on the right changes each time a new version builds.

Nothing opens in Sessions. The work runs in a session Slate keeps in the background, under your own AI tool and account. See the full session opens it if you want to look.

A mini app belongs to a project, and reads only that project.

Changing one

  • Ask in the chat. "Make the buttons green", "add a filter for this week". The same chat goes on while its session is open; New chat starts over.
  • Edit the files. Files shows the app's folder: a tree and an editor. Add a file (a path like lib/format.ts makes its folder), remove one, and press Save (⌘S). Slate builds the app. If the build fails, nothing is saved: your edits stay, and each error opens its file at its line. Only the newest version of your own app can be edited.

The bar above the app has Reload, Full size (Esc returns) and a button that hides the chat. While a mini app is open, the Artifacts list steps aside.

What it can do

It reads freely inside its project: pages, memories, sessions and their counts, and routines. It keeps its own data on this Mac (up to 1 MB), never synced.

Anything else needs a permission, which the app lists in its slate.json with a reason. Slate asks you the first time, on a card it draws outside the app:

PermissionWhat it allows
Read your TasksEvery open item in Tasks.
Change your TasksAdd your own items, set reminders, mark items done.
Save memoriesSave memories in this project. Every AI tool can recall them.
Write pagesMake pages and add text to pages in this project.
Run routinesRun a routine. Slate asks you each time, naming it.
Open AI sessionsOpen a session with a prompt typed in. You press Send.

Even when allowed, a memory or page write shows you its words on a card before anything is saved. Permissions lists what the app asked for and lets you take any of it back. An app cannot delete anything.

What it cannot do

  • Reach the internet. No requests, sockets, images, fonts or scripts from anywhere. No WebRTC, no resource hints, no frames, no popups. If it tries to leave for another address, Slate closes it.
  • Read outside its project, or anything of a teammate's.
  • Act on its own where it matters: a routine, a session, a memory or page text all wait for you.

In a shared workspace, a teammate sees your mini app as its built page, with nothing answered: it never reads their store.

For the AI tool building it

An app is index.html with <script type="module" src="./App.tsx"></script>, then .tsx .ts .jsx .js .css .json .md .svg files in any folders. It may import its own files and react, react-dom/client, lucide-react, @slate/sdk (the calls), @slate/sdk/react (useQuery, useStore) and @slate/ui (Slate's own buttons, inputs, cards, rows and states, in both themes). The tool sends files with publish_artifact; get_artifact on a mini app returns its files and the whole API as sdk.

Limits

  • Up to 300 files per app.
  • An app's own data: 1 MB.
  • A toast at most once a second.

Slate is in alpha. Your data stays on your machine.
