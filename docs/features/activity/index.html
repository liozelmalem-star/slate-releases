Activity & Analytics

Slate captures your AI sessions as you work. The Activity page is where that history becomes something you can read: what you did today, which projects you touched, and how your work is trending.

Everything here is derived from data already on your machine. Nothing is fetched, and nothing is sent out to compute it.

Where the numbers come from

Two things feed this page.

The first is session capture: a watcher records your AI sessions from Claude, Codex, Gemini, Cursor and OpenCode as pages, with structured properties (prompt, summary, tool, tokens, model, branch, sessionId). The Activity page reads those.

The second is the activity_stats tool, which returns session totals (all-time and this week), a per-tool breakdown, the top projects by session count, and a memory block — the same tool your AI clients can call over the MCP server.

Sessions have been captured two ways over Slate's history. To avoid double-counting, the Activity page deduplicates legacy session pages against mission-model conversationIds, so a session recorded under both shapes still counts once.
