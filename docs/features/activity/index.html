Features

Activity & Analytics

Slate captures your AI sessions as you work. The Activity page is where that history becomes something you can read: what you did today, which projects you touched, and how your work is trending. Everything here is derived from data already on your machine.

The dashboard

Activity is a board of widgets. These are on it out of the box:

WidgetWhat it tells you
Sessions over timeThe trend, over the range you picked
RecordsYour busiest day, best week, longest focus and favourite tool
TimelineRecent sessions and facts, newest first
Last 13 weeksA calendar grid of activity density
Time of day · WeekdayWhen during the day and the week you work
By tool, By project, By branchSessions ranked by the tool that ran them and the project and branch they ran in

Eight more are one click away under Edit: Sessions (the full list), Momentum, By model, Memory facts, Quick stats, and three readings of the memory layer — Memory contract, Recall quality and Corpus health.

Edit also reorders widgets and hides the ones you don't want, and a layout you like can be kept as a named view. The range switch at the top covers 7d, 30d, 90d, 1y and All; drag across the trend chart to zoom into a narrower window. Clicking a row in any breakdown filters the whole board to it.

Where the numbers come from

Two things feed this page.

The first is session capture: the watcher records your AI sessions from Claude, Codex, Gemini, Cursor and OpenCode as pages, with structured properties (prompt, summary, tool, tokens, model, branch, sessionId). The Activity page reads those.

The second is the activity_stats tool, which returns session totals (all-time and this week), a per-tool breakdown, the top projects by session count, and a memory block — the same tool your AI clients can call over the MCP server.
